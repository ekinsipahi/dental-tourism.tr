## The short answer

Yes. Send us what you have, a panoramic X-ray or a tomography (CBCT) scan, on WhatsApp or by e-mail. We look at it and tell you, in writing, what is likely to be possible, what is not, and how many visits you should plan for. This is a preliminary assessment, it is free of charge, and it is not the final treatment plan. The final plan is made in the clinic, after an examination, and there is a good reason for that, which we explain below.

Thousands of international patients have gone through this process with our clinic in Şişli, Istanbul. They write to us from Russia, Canada, the United States, the United Kingdom, Moldova, Belarus, Arabic-speaking countries and across Europe, and the steps are the same for everyone.

**Key points**

- Send a panoramic X-ray if you have one. If implants are a possibility, the decision rests on a tomography (CBCT) scan.
- You receive a written preliminary assessment on WhatsApp or e-mail. The final plan is made after an intraoral examination.
- Whether a tooth can be saved cannot always be judged from an X-ray. That decision is made in the chair.
- Tell us about your health and your medicines before you travel. Several of them change the plan or mean we consult your own doctor first.
- Implants are rarely finished in one trip. Plan for two visits, two to three months apart.
- We write in Turkish and English. A Russian-speaking interpreter is available.

## What to send us

**Your X-ray.** If you have a panoramic X-ray, send it. If you have a tomography scan, send that too; it is the best possible starting point, because it shows the density of your bone, and that is what the decision about an implant rests on. If you have neither, tell us what the problem is and we will say what is needed.

**Your health and your medicines.** This is not a formality. Diabetes, medicines that act on bone, low vitamin D and the drugs given for osteoporosis all influence whether an implant heals into the bone. Blood thinners such as warfarin (Coumadin) and corticosteroids may mean we need to consult your own doctor before we proceed. Heart conditions, high blood pressure, Paget's disease and other bone diseases, and autoimmune diseases must be mentioned as well. We need this list before we start, not in the chair.

**How much you smoke.** Honestly. If you smoke little or not at all, we see no reason for an implant to fail because of it; how much you smoke is one of the things we weigh when we plan.

**Earlier work done elsewhere.** If you already have implants from another clinic, send the brand or the identification stickers you were given, together with your X-rays. We identify the implant first; the rest follows from that.

**What you want.** Tell us what bothers you and what you hope to achieve. Our answer will be about what your mouth allows, which is not always the same thing.

Your message is read and answered by the clinic's dentists or their assistants, not by a call centre. From what you send, we can tell you what is likely: which teeth look as if they can be kept, whether implants look possible, which options are realistic for your case, and how many visits to plan for. We put this in writing, so that you have something to compare and to think about.

What we cannot do from an X-ray is give you a final answer on a tooth. Whether a tooth can be kept or has to be extracted cannot always be judged from a panoramic X-ray or a tomography scan; some of that only shows itself when the tooth is examined in the mouth. Bone density, which decides whether and where an implant can go, needs a tomography scan; a panoramic X-ray alone is not enough for that. So the preliminary assessment is exactly what the name says. It tells you what is likely. It does not commit you, and it does not commit us.

## Why the final plan is made in the clinic

Before we commit to any treatment, and before an implant in particular, an intraoral examination is a condition. That is why we do not send a final treatment plan before you travel, even though it would be easier to sell. A plan made remotely has to be revised in the chair more often than patients expect, and a plan that is revised after you have flown in is not a plan you can rely on.

If you compare clinics, this is a useful question to ask each of them: is the final plan made after an examination in the chair, or is it sold to me remotely? We have written about the other questions worth asking in [our article on whether dental implants in Turkey are safe](/blog/safety-and-quality/is-it-safe-to-get-dental-implants-in-turkey/).

## If implants are on the table: what the scan decides

For implants, the tomography scan is where the planning really starts. We ask every implant patient for one; if you do not have a scan, one is taken before any decision is made. What it shows decides the rest:

- **Enough bone:** a temporary tooth can sometimes be fitted within the first week (immediate loading). Even then, the final teeth come later.
- **Not enough bone for immediate loading:** the implant heals first, about two months in the lower jaw and three in the upper. In practice we wait three months for both, because the fit of the final prosthesis depends on it.
- **Too little bone for a standard implant:** we do sinus lifts with grafting; if the bone is still insufficient, short implants are an option, and for a back jaw where the bone has resorbed, All-on-4 can be considered. We generally do not do membrane-based bone augmentation.

This is why one trip is usually not enough for implants, and why we say so before you book anything. We currently work with the S.I.N. implant system, chosen for its quality; Nobel Biocare, Straumann and other premium systems are available at a considerably higher cost and are discussed case by case. Whatever is placed, you receive the implant's identification stickers. The treatment itself is described on our [dental implants page](/treatments/dental-implants/).

## What happens after the assessment

You read what we have written, ask whatever you like, and decide in your own time. If you go ahead, we agree the dates around your travel plans, with the number of visits and the gap between them already known.

On your first visit you are examined in the clinic; a tomography scan is taken if you do not have one. The final plan is made at that examination and discussed with you before anything starts. While you are in Istanbul you reach the clinic directly if anything comes up. After you go home we stay in touch and ask how you are doing; your own dentist can take over the follow-up if you prefer, and if you wish we explain the case to them directly. Your records and images are kept, and we share them with you or your dentist when you ask.

- A list of your medical conditions and the medicines you take.
- How much you smoke.
- For implants or other work done elsewhere: the brand, the identification stickers and earlier X-rays.
- The language you prefer to write in.
