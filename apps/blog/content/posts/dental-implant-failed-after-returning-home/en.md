## The short answer

Write to us. Send photos of the implant and the gum around it, and an X-ray if you can get one, and we tell you what to do. If the implant has failed, we place a new one and do not charge for it. The only exception is a failure the patient has caused, for example by smoking.

The possibility of failure always exists, with any implant, in any country. What matters is what happens next, and whether anyone is still answering you after you have flown home. Below is how we handle it, so that you know before you travel, not after.

**Key points**

- Four signs mean write to us at once: red, inflamed gums around the implant, a sunken or receded look under the tooth, an implant that moves, or a screw that has come out.
- The first step from home is a message to the clinic with photos and, if possible, a panoramic X-ray; a dentist near you can take that X-ray and check the implant.
- Check-ups: normally every six months and at least once a year; a panoramic X-ray shows what is needed.
- If an implant fails, we place a new one and do not charge for it. Patient-caused failures, such as smoking, are the exception.
- A broken temporary tooth is not a failure. Tell us and it is sorted out.
- Keep the identification stickers of your implant; they let any dentist identify it years from now.
- After you go home we stay in touch and ask how you are doing, more than once.

## Which signs should make you write to us at once?

There are four. Red, inflamed gums around the implant: this is peri-implantitis, inflammation of the tissue around the implant. A sunken or receded appearance under the tooth, as if the gum and bone were pulling away from it. An implant that moves. And an implant whose screw has come out.

These are either signs of failure or signs of a problem that leads to failure. The second group is the reason to write early: a problem that is still developing is easier to deal with than one that has finished developing. Do not wait to see whether it settles. Take photos in good light, and write to us the same day.

## What is the first step from home?

Write to the clinic. Send the photos, describe what you feel, and send an X-ray if you can get one where you live. A panoramic X-ray (OPG) taken by a dentist near you shows what we need to see. We look at it and tell you what to do next; you do not need to decide on your own whether it is serious, that is our job.

Your job in the meantime is simpler: look after the implants well. Clean them as you were shown, and keep answering when we ask how you are doing, because we will ask. Your own dentist can be involved at any point; see below.

## Who pays if the implant has to be replaced?

If an implant fails, we place a new one and do not charge for it. That is the rule, and it is the same rule we state before you travel. The exception is a failure caused by the patient's own situation: a patient who smokes, for example, is not covered by that rule, and we say so in advance rather than afterwards. This is also why we ask how much you smoke when we plan, and why we tell you honestly if it changes the risk.

Two things are not failures. A temporary tooth can break; temporaries are for the smile while the implant heals, not for hard food, and a broken one is replaced without drama. And the final teeth are not something we expect to lose: we make prostheses to last for many years, which is one of the reasons we do not hurry the healing time before they are made. If a final crown does break, it can be remade. In general we do that without charge, but it depends on how it broke, and that is a conversation rather than a rule; a crown that was used to open bottles is a different case from one that failed on its own, and we can tell the difference.

## What if something happens while you are still in Istanbul?

Then you reach the clinic directly. If painkillers are needed, they are prescribed before you leave the chair, so you are not left looking for a pharmacy with a prescription you do not have. We have described what the first week looks like in [how many visits dental implants in Turkey really take](/blog/planning-your-trip/dental-implants-in-turkey-how-many-visits/).

After you fly home we do not wait for you to write. We stay in touch and ask, more than once, how you are doing. If something is wrong, you hear from us what to do.

## Why do implants fail at all?

Because the possibility always exists. Some failures come from complications that have to do with the patient's own situation rather than the surgery. Diabetes, medicines that act on bone, low vitamin D and the drugs given for osteoporosis influence whether an implant heals into the bone. Blood thinners such as warfarin (Coumadin) and corticosteroids may mean we consult your own doctor first. Heart conditions, high blood pressure, Paget's disease and other bone diseases, and autoimmune diseases have to be known before anything is placed. Smoking is on the same list; if you smoke little or not at all, we see no reason for an implant to fail because of it.

This is why we ask for all of it before we start, and why the plan is made after a tomography scan and an examination in the clinic rather than over the phone. The whole of that process is in [our article on whether dental implants in Turkey are safe](/blog/safety-and-quality/is-it-safe-to-get-dental-implants-in-turkey/).

## Can your own dentist at home take over?

Yes, if that is what you prefer. Your dentist can do the follow-up or the crown, and if you wish we explain the case to them directly. Your records and images are kept, and we share them with you or with your dentist when you ask. For routine check-ups your own dentist is well placed: normally every six months, at least once a year, and in some severe cases every three months. A panoramic X-ray (OPG) shows what is needed; send it to us as well and we look at it too.

Whatever implant system is placed, you receive its identification stickers: the brand, the type and size, the lot number, the expiry date and a barcode or QR code. Keep them with your other medical papers. If anything ever needs to be done to the implant, here or anywhere else, those stickers tell the dentist exactly what is in your bone and which parts fit it. They are also how the implant can be verified as genuine.

## What if the failed implant was placed by another clinic?

We treat those patients too, and the first thing we do is find out the brand. If the brand is known, we order the matching parts and can continue on the existing implant. If one implant has failed, we replace it. If all of them have failed, we stop and ask why; sometimes the honest answer is that implants should not have been indicated for that patient in the first place, and that has to be assessed before anything new goes in.

An implant that cannot be used can sometimes be left buried in the bone, "put to sleep", while a new implant of our own system is placed beside it. When a bridge is planned, two different implant systems can be combined with the right parts. Bring whatever you have: the stickers or the brand of your implants, and your X-rays.

## Before you travel: how we reduce the chance of getting here

The best time to deal with a failed implant is before it is placed. Send your X-ray or tomography scan before you travel and we tell you in writing what is likely to be possible; [how that preliminary assessment works](/blog/planning-your-trip/send-your-dental-x-ray-for-a-preliminary-assessment/) is explained separately. Tell us about your health and your smoking. Accept that the final plan is made in the chair, after an examination, and that the implant heals for two to three months before the final teeth. The [dental implants page](/treatments/dental-implants/) describes the treatment itself, and our [aftercare guide](/guide/dental-guarantee-aftercare-turkey/) describes how follow-up works from abroad.

## After you get home: a short checklist

- Look after the implants well, as you were shown in the clinic.
- Keep the identification stickers with your medical papers.
- Red gums, a sunken look under the tooth, movement, or a screw that has come out: photos and a message to us the same day.
- Check-ups every six months, at least once a year, with your own dentist if that is easier; a panoramic X-ray shows what is needed.
- A broken temporary is not an emergency. Tell us.
- When we write to ask how you are doing, answer. It is how problems are caught early.
