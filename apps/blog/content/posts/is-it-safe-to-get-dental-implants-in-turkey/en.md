## The short answer

Yes. Having [dental implants](/treatments/dental-implants/) placed in Turkey is a reasonable decision, and with the right dentist and an experienced clinic there is generally nothing to be afraid of. Thousands of international patients have been treated in our clinic in Şişli, Istanbul.

The risk is not the country. It is the clinic: who looks at your jaw before anything is drilled, who places the implant, which system goes into the bone, how much time the plan allows for healing, and whether anyone is still answering you after you fly home. Below is how we handle each of these points, so that you can hold any clinic against the same list.

**Key points**

- The decision to place an implant rests on a tomography (CBCT) scan and an examination in the clinic, not on a phone call.
- Diabetes, bone medication, blood thinners, corticosteroids and several other conditions change the plan; they must be disclosed first.
- Instruments are autoclaved and opened from sealed pouches for each patient.
- You receive the identification stickers of your implant, so any dentist can identify it later.
- One trip is usually not enough: unless there is enough bone for immediate loading, the implant heals for two to three months before the final teeth.
- If an implant fails, we place a new one and do not charge for it.

## Start with the reviews, and read the bad ones

Before you look at prices or photos, look at the reviews, including the negative ones. Our rule of thumb: a clinic with 70 or more reviews and only a few bad ones is generally a safe choice. A clinic with many bad reviews is not, however good its photos look. What patients complain about tells you more than the average star rating.

## What we look at before placing an implant

If a panoramic X-ray exists we look at it, but the decision rests on a tomography scan (CBCT), because that is what shows the density of your bone. We ask every implant patient for one. If you already have a scan, send it to us before you travel; that is the best starting point.

Before you travel we talk through your case on WhatsApp or e-mail. We look at what you send and tell you, in writing, what is likely to be possible. What you do not get at that stage is a final treatment plan, and there is a reason for it: the final decision is made in the chair, after an intraoral examination. Whether a tooth can be kept or has to be extracted cannot always be judged from a panoramic X-ray or a tomography scan. An examination in the clinic is therefore a condition before any implant is placed.

## Tell us about your health; it changes the plan

Several conditions and medicines affect whether an implant heals into the bone. Diabetes, medicines that act on bone, low vitamin D and the drugs given for osteoporosis all influence implant success. If you take blood thinners such as warfarin (Coumadin) or corticosteroids, we may need to consult your own doctor before we proceed; the same applies to osteoporosis medication.

Heart conditions, diabetes, high blood pressure, Paget's disease and other bone diseases, autoimmune diseases: if anything in your history puts a second question mark over an implant, we need to know it before we start. An implant should not be placed without this information, and no clinic should place one without asking for it.

Whatever system is used, you receive the implant's identification stickers. They carry the brand, the implant's original identification (type and size), the lot number, the expiry date and a barcode or QR code, which is also how the implant can be verified as genuine. Keep them. Years from now, any dentist in any country can identify your implant from them and order compatible parts. We do this so that the implant never becomes a problem for you later.

## Can everything be done in one trip? Usually not

Finishing implants and the final teeth in a single trip is generally not possible. When it is forced, the fit of the prosthesis on top becomes the second question mark, and that is not a risk we take.

Immediate loading, a temporary tooth fitted within the first week, is possible only if there is enough bone. If there is not, the implant has to heal first: about two months in the lower jaw and three in the upper. In practice most dentists wait three months for both jaws, because the fit of the final prosthesis depends on it. Plan for two visits, and be careful with any clinic that promises everything in one.

## Smoking and the other reasons implants fail

In our experience, if you smoke little or not at all, there is no reason for an implant to fail. The health conditions and medicines described above are the other factors, which is why we insist on knowing them. Tell us honestly how much you smoke; it is part of the planning.

## What happens if an implant fails after you go home

The possibility of failure always exists. It depends on complications, and some of them are caused by the patient's own situation rather than by the surgery. That is why we ask about your health and your smoking before we start, and why we tell you in advance what happens if it does fail.

We stay in touch after you return and ask, more than once, how you are doing. If something is wrong, we help. If an implant fails, we place a new one and do not charge for it. It is that simple. ## Before you contact any clinic: six things to ask yourself

This part is not dental advice. It is plain common sense, and it applies to every clinic, including ours.

1. **What is the name of the dentist who will treat me?** Look the name up online and check whether they have published academic work. When you arrive, make sure the person treating you really is that dentist. If it is someone else, ask for their full name and look them up too. If there is no time for that, there is no need to take an unnecessary risk.
2. **What do the Google reviews say, and how many of them are bad?** Fake reviews are often impossible to tell apart, but a large number of bad reviews tells you everything about a clinic.
3. **Are they talking about extracting teeth and placing implants, or about saving the teeth you have?** This is the single most important point. Once a tooth is gone, it does not come back.
4. **Did they say one thing on the phone and another in the clinic?** If a clinic agrees to what you asked for on the phone and then, once you are in the chair, proposes extractions and implants or cutting down all your teeth, with figures several times higher than discussed, that is rarely a dentist's decision. It is the behaviour of a clinic with no dentist behind it.
5. **Does the clinic have a Google profile at all?** People who are not the dentists they claim to be, or not dentists at all, can offer teeth far cheaper than they should cost. How long that work will last, and in what kind of environment it was done, should make you think twice. Anyone can say "I am a doctor".
6. **Is it a well-known clinic, or one that carries a dentist's own name?** Choose one of the two. Do not lose your health because something was cheap.

## Questions to ask the clinic before you book

- How many reviews do you have, and what do the negative ones say?
- Will you ask for a tomography scan before you decide anything?
- Is the final plan made after an examination in the chair, or is it sold to me remotely?
- Who exactly will place my implant, and who takes over if my case is difficult?
- Which implant system will you use, and will I receive its identification labels?
- Have you asked about my medical conditions and the medicines I take?
- How many trips will I need, and how long between the implant and the final teeth?
- What happens if the implant fails after I return home, and who pays for the replacement?

If a clinic answers all eight without hesitation, you are probably in good hands. For the wider picture, see our guides to [dental care in Turkey](/guide/dental-care-turkey/) and [getting your teeth done in Turkey](/guide/getting-teeth-done-in-turkey/).
